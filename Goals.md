I want to create a video game that is in the arena-shooter adjacent genre. A few games of influence would be Brotato, Pickle Pete, Vampire Survivors.

I want to make mine a fantasy theme, and I think I want to do a few things a little different than other games like this, by integrating RPG types of features. For example, armor slots, trinkets, rings, etc.

1. I want the player to be able to choose different classes to play as, with very different play styles. My thoughts on these are fantasy/dnd type classes.
   - Fighter - A class focused on weapons. Decent armor/health
   - Mage - Focused on magic. low armor/health
   - Warlock - Magic and focus is health draining/life stealing
   - Necromancer - Minions and magic

I think I also like the idea of adding RPG elements like a spell list/skill tree, and at the start of each game, the player can select 3-4 options from their spell list. For example maybe the mage wants to focus on a frost/cold build, or a fire/burning build.

I think I also want to add potions/buffs. So maybe health potions are purchased, or dropped during games, and they can choose to use them during the rounds.

I think game play is similar to Brotato. There are maybe rounds that last maybe a minute or two. Enemies spawn from around the map. In between each round the player get's to manage inventory/rewards. I'm not sure yet how I want to do that piece. I'm thinking either currency is gained throughout each round and options are availalbe for purchase, or items drop during the round and the user can choose which to equip. Or maybe even a combination of the two, and items they don't want can be sold and used to purchase other items.

## Decisions so far (prototype — revisit after playtesting)

- **Currency:** gold. Enemies drop it, and the player has to walk over it to collect it (Pickup Range is an upgradable stat). Gold left on the floor when a wave ends goes into a **bag** (same as Brotato's bag and Pickle Pete's jar, for familiarity): each pickup in later waves pays double, drawing the extra from the bag, until it's empty. The bag can grow across waves and never expires.
- **Between waves:** a shop offering 4 random items from a catalog, with rerolls that get more expensive each time and prices that rise slightly each wave. Items are permanent stat modifiers for the rest of the run. Item drops and selling aren't built yet (replacing gear refunds half its price, see below).
- **Run structure:** 20 waves (Brotato-length, about 19 minutes; was 5 in the first prototype), of increasing length (30s up to 60s from wave 4). Each wave increases the spawn rate and enemy health (+35%/wave, linear), damage (+15%/wave, a percentage so weak and strong enemies keep their difference) and speed (+5%/wave, capped at +40% so every enemy stays slower than every class). HP refills at the start of each wave. Decided before the balance pass, since every balance number depends on run length.
- **Weapons and spells:** every class's equipped weapon is its basic attack and fires constantly on its own (aiming at the nearest enemy). Spells and abilities are stronger options on longer cooldowns, kept in hotkey slots 1–4. Each slot can auto-cast or be cast on demand (manual casts aim at the mouse). Keys after 4 are for consumables (5, 6 so far).
- **Classes so far (8 spells each, pick 3):** Fighter (Sword; spells Whirlwind, Ground Slam, Charge, Shield Wall, War Cry, Throwing Axe, Shockwave, Second Wind), Mage (Wand; spells Fireball, Frost Nova, Ice Bolt, Chain Lightning, Meteor, Arcane Missiles, Blizzard, Blink), Warlock (Hex Rod; Drain Life, Curse of Agony, Shadow Bolt, Crimson Nova, Blood Pact, Siphoning Aura, Corruption, Vampire Bats) and Necromancer (Bone Wand; Raise Skeletons, Bone Golem, Grave Curse, Bone Spear, Bone Nova, Skeletal Mage, Bone Armor, Corpse Explosion). **Base toughness (HP / armor):** Fighter 24 / 1, Mage 24 / 1, Necromancer 24 / 1, Warlock 21 / 0 (plus life steal). Set by the class balance pass: the Fighter lost HP and weapon damage (it won every top build), the casters gained HP (two Brute hits used to kill them by wave 6). No class is one-shot by a mid-wave Brute; casters have 1 armor because flat armor takes most off the many small hits (Runners, Archers, Grunts) while HP is what survives a big one.
- **Spell mechanics added with the 8-spell lists (first guesses, revisit after playtest):** *Stun* (Shockwave) stops movement and shooting; knockback resistance shortens it (Brute keeps 65%). *Barrier* (Bone Armor) absorbs damage after armor until used up or expired; recasting refreshes, it doesn't stack, and it clears at wave start like buffs. *Spreading DoT* (Corruption): when the host dies, the DoT jumps to the 2 nearest enemies not already carrying a spreading one, at full duration; it keeps spreading while it kills. *Ranged minions* exist (Skeletal Mage) and the first one is **not targetable**, as a contrast to the tanking skeletons. *Corpse Explosion* only detonates skeletons (golem and mages are excluded), so it's a combo with Raise Skeletons / Grave Curse. Life steal stays Warlock-only: the two new Warlock steal spells are Siphoning Aura and Vampire Bats.
- **Life steal:** a percent of damage dealt that heals you. Each ability has a base (Hex Rod 8%, Drain Life 40%…) and the **Life Steal** stat adds to all of them, like Crit Chance. Overkill doesn't count, DoT ticks do. Capped at 5 HP per second from all sources (Brotato caps it too), so area spells can't full-heal in one hit. **Life steal is a Warlock thing:** it comes from Warlock spells (Drain Life, Crimson Nova, Curse of Agony, and Blood Pact's +15% Life Steal buff) and Warlock weapons. The shared shop sells no life steal: Vampiric Fang left the catalog, and Bloodstone Ring and Blood-Soaked Robes became plain HP/Damage/Attack Speed gear (same `Id`s, so saves still load).
- **Defensive spells and auto-cast:** a defensive spell earns its slot by firing when it matters and by scaling with the character. Auto-cast decides from small per-spell conditions in the data, not a global AI: a crowd within some distance (`MinTargets` within the radius or `CastWhenEnemiesWithin`), being hit in the last moment (`CastWhenHitWithin`), or a health threshold (`CastBelowHealth`; Blood Pact's HP cost waits until you stay above half). Barriers and heals are a fraction of max HP (Shield Wall 30%, Second Wind 35%) so they keep up with enemy damage (+15%/wave) as HP items stack; Bone Armor keeps its flat 10 for now. Blink's auto-cast is an escape: only when cornered or just hit, to the safest spot in range, and not at all when no spot is clearly safer.
- **Damage over time:** any ability can apply a DoT (curses, Shadow Bolt; fire burns later). One DoT per enemy: the strongest wins, and a weaker one doesn't extend it.
- **Minions (Necromancer):** allied units with simple AI (attack the nearest enemy near the player, otherwise follow). They expire on a timer. Each minion type has a **Targetable** toggle (on for skeletons and the golem): enemies then chase whichever is nearest, the player or a targetable minion, their contact hurts it and enemy shots hit it, so minions tank. A non-targetable type is ignored by enemies and can't be hurt; only its timer ends it. Each type has a cap (6 skeletons, 1 golem); summoning past it crumbles the oldest. They come from summon spells and from enemies killed while marked (Grave Curse, Reaper's Sickle, Skull Staff). Minion damage scales with Damage% plus a **Minion Damage** stat. Minions don't life steal. **Minion HP scales with the wave** at the same rate as enemy damage (+15%/wave, `WaveSettings.MinionHealthMultiplier`), so a minion survives the same number of hits in every wave; minion damage doesn't scale with waves (it has the Damage% / Minion Damage stats, like the player's attacks).
- **Look (placeholder, not final):** characters are front-facing (Brotato-style, not top-down rotating), authored facing right and mirrored by movement direction, with a floor shadow. Each enemy type keeps its old body color so types stay recognizable. The final art style (pixel vs vector) is still open; it plugs into the same `Sprite` fields.
- **Spell selection:** at run start, after picking a class, the player picks 3 spells from the class's list (`ClassData.SpellPicks`). Pick order sets hotkeys 1–3. Slot 4 is left open for now, so spells could also be gained during the run later (the "mix" option). Each class has 8 spells, shown as a 4×2 grid.
- **Buffs:** temporary stat bonuses (War Cry, Shield Wall; potions later) sit on top of the run's stats and expire on a timer. Recasting refreshes the timer instead of stacking. All buffs clear at the start of each wave.
- **Healing:** potions, plus life steal. A health potion (+10 HP) on Space, one free charge per wave, refilled at each wave start. HP still refills fully between waves.
  - **Hearts heal when you're hurt, else fill a reserve potion.** Enemies sometimes drop a heart (+2). Below full HP it heals you directly (any part past full HP goes into the reserve); at full HP it pours into a second, slowly-filled potion. The reserve holds one potion's heal (10, grows with Potion Heal), keeps between waves, and doesn't become a charge. Hearts stay on the floor only while you're at full HP *and* the reserve is full; a heart worth more than the room left tops it up and the rest is lost.
  - **Space drinks in this order:** a full reserve first (to make room for more hearts), then a full potion (the free charge, then bought ones), and only when none are left, the whole reserve, whatever its fill. Nothing happens at full HP, so a misclick doesn't waste a potion.
- **Consumables:** bought on a potion shelf under the shop's offers, always in stock (not rerolled, not taking an offer slot), price rising with the wave like items. They're kept until drunk, with a carry limit each. Shop only: they don't drop during waves. Health Potion (extra full charge, carry 2), Potion of Fury (+30% Damage for 10s, key 5), Potion of Swiftness (+35% Move Speed, +60 Pickup Range for 10s, key 6). Buff potions are timed buffs: drinking one that's active refreshes it, and they clear at wave start like other buffs. A bigger heal is an item, not a consumable: the **Potion Heal** stat (Alchemist's Flask trinket, +4) adds to the potion and the reserve.
- **Equipment (two kinds of item, Brotato-style split):** *gear* goes in 6 slots (Main Hand, Off Hand, Armor, Ring ×2, Amulet), one item each. *Trinkets* have no slot and stack without limit (Whetstone, Rabbit's Foot…). Gear is limited, so it gets bigger numbers per item than trinkets.
  - Buying gear for a full slot replaces the old piece, which refunds half its base price. No inventory or stash. When both ring slots are full, the player picks which ring to replace.
  - The main-hand item is the weapon, i.e. the basic attack. Each class starts with its weapon (Sword, Wand) and can buy its own others (`ClassData.Weapons`). Weapons are class-locked for now so classes keep their identity. Shared weapons could go into the catalog later. **Every class has 6 weapons, 3 melee and 3 ranged**, so any class can play either way, flavored to the class (a Fighter's Crossbow, a Mage's Frostbrand). Weapons say which they are on shop cards, tooltips and the HUD.
  - Two-handed weapons (Greatsword, Staff) also fill the off hand. Buying an off-hand item while holding one puts the starting weapon back in the main hand.
  - The shop doesn't offer gear you're already wearing (except rings).
- **Item rarity (Diablo/Brotato-style ladder):** Common (white), Uncommon (green), Rare (blue), Epic (purple). Rarity is rolled per shop offer, so any catalog item or class weapon can show up at any tier; rerolls roll rarity again. Higher tiers multiply the item's **positive** stats (×1 / 1.25 / 1.5 / 2, rounded, always at least +1 over Common) and its price (×1 / 1.2 / 1.45 / 1.8, so rarer items are slightly better value per gold). Downsides (e.g. Chainmail's −5% Move Speed) don't scale: a rarer copy is strictly better. Weapons also hit harder (×1 / 1.15 / 1.3 / 1.5 basic attack damage, multiplied with the Damage stat). Odds rise with the wave: about 82/15/3/0% at the first shop, 36/39/18/8% at the last. Starting weapons are always Common; consumables have no rarity. Refunds are half the rarity-scaled base price. Trinkets of different rarities stack and simply add up. The shop still hides gear you're wearing (any rarity), except rings. The career heirloom keeps its rarity (the base for future heirloom upgrades).
- **Class-restricted shop items:** the shared catalog can tag an item with the classes it's offered to (`ItemData.Classes`, empty = every class), so an item whose only value is a class mechanic isn't a dead offer for everyone else. Bone Charm, Book of the Dead and Phylactery (Minion Damage) are Necromancer-only. Each other class also gets one signature off-hand for flavor: War Banner (Fighter), Spell Focus (Mage), Blood Grimoire (Warlock). The shop still sells no life steal stat (see Life steal). Class weapons stay in `ClassData.Weapons`.
- **Class balance target:** classes should be about equal **on their best builds with good shopping**, not on average: per class, the top 20% of builds (11 of 56) in a `tools/sweep.py` run with `--shop=smart` (Autoplay's smart movement and auto-cast), replayed on fresh seeds. Aim for those win rates within ~15 points of each other, around 50–70%. A class that wins every top build is nerfed rather than every other class buffed, and late waves aren't made harder for everyone while only one class saturates. The average over all builds is context (it shows how forgiving a class is), not the target.
- **Armor** is a flat reduction to each hit (minimum 1 damage).
- **Defensive and tempo stats (Brotato/LoL style):**
  - **Cooldown Reduction** (%) shortens **spell** cooldowns only; the weapon's rate is Attack Speed's job, so the two stats don't overlap. Capped at **50%** (spells at most twice as often). Cards say "caps at 50%"; past the cap the stats panel shows "(capped)".
  - **HP Regen** is **HP per 10 seconds** (whole numbers, so rarity scaling works; +2 per 10s ≈ 12 HP over a 60s wave, enough to matter for 16–30 HP pools). It heals HP only and never fills the potion reserve (hearts do that). Uncapped for now.
  - **Dodge** (%) avoids a hit entirely, contact or enemy shot. Capped at **60%** like Brotato. A dodge starts the same i-frames as a hit, so touching a crowd rolls once per 0.5s instead of every frame (otherwise dodge would be useless against contact). It is rolled before armor, so the two stack: dodge skips a hit, armor shrinks the ones that land.
  - Shop: Hourglass (+8% CDR trinket), Sage's Amulet (+15% CDR, +5% Damage), Mending Herb (+2 Regen trinket), Ring of Regeneration (+3 Regen, +3 Max HP), Feather Charm (+5% Dodge trinket), Shadow Cloak (armor, +10% Dodge, +5% Move Speed). Career talents Focus (+3% CDR), Evasion (+2% Dodge) (5 ranks each), Recovery (+1 Regen, 3 ranks): 46 ranks for 24 points at the cap.
- **Crits (Brotato-style):** each weapon and spell has its own base crit chance (5% default) and crit multiplier (×1.5 default; heavy hits like Sword and Ground Slam ×2). The player has a global **Crit Chance** stat only, added to every ability's base and capped at 100%. No global crit damage stat: fast and slow weapons need different multipliers, and chance × damage both being buyable runs away in a shop where items stack. Revisit a D3-style additive Crit Damage stat if equipment slots add a gear budget. Crit is rolled per enemy on impact; damage reservations use the non-crit damage.
- **Progression modes:** a mode select screen comes first. **Arcade** is a single fresh run with nothing saved. **Career** gives each class one persistent character (one save file, `user://career.json`, slot-less for now). Career runs keep the same 20-wave structure and difficulty. XP for 20-wave runs: 0.1 per kill (rounded per run), 20 per wave cleared, 100 for a victory, so a full victory (~3000 kills) is worth ~800 XP, about two old 5-wave victories.
  - **XP and levels:** every career run banks XP, win or lose: 1 per kill, 20 per wave cleared, +50 for a victory (a won run is ~350–450 XP, an early death ~70–110). Level 2 costs 60 XP and each level 40 more than the last; max level 25. Each level after the first is one **talent point**.
  - **Talents:** permanent stat bonuses for that class's career runs, 1 point per rank: Toughness +2 Max HP, Might +3% Damage, Haste +3% Attack Speed, Fleet Foot +3% Move Speed (5 ranks each), Iron Skin +1 Armor (3), Precision +2% Crit Chance, Magnetism +12 Pickup Range, Focus +3% Cooldown Reduction, Evasion +2% Dodge (5 each), Recovery +1 HP Regen per 10s (3). 46 ranks but only 24 points at the cap, so a maxed character still chooses. Reset is free at any time (no respec cost), so experimenting is safe.
  - **Heirloom:** after a career victory the player may keep one worn piece of gear (not the starting weapon); it starts equipped in every later run of that class until replaced. One heirloom per character. Defeat keeps XP and record but never changes the heirloom.
  - **Save IDs:** the save references classes, items and talents by an explicit `Id` export, never by file name. An `Id` must never change once shipped; files can be renamed freely.
  - Abandoning a run (quitting the app, or Abandon run / Retry / Quit in the pause menu) banks nothing, not even XP for the kills so far. The pause menu's confirm prompt says so in career mode. Gold, trinkets and potions never carry over.
- **Enemy roster so far:** Grunt (basic chaser), Runner (fast and fragile, from wave 2), Archer (keeps its distance and shoots dodgeable bolts, from wave 3), Brute (slow, tanky, resists knockback, hits hard, from wave 4). Each spawn is a weighted random pick from the types unlocked so far.
- **Menus and quality of life:**
  - **Esc** opens a pause menu during a wave or the shop: Resume, Settings, Retry (same class and spells), Abandon run (back to class select in the same mode), Quit game. The three that end the run ask first. **Tab** opens a read-only gear screen during a wave (equipment and trinkets with tooltips, potions, active buffs, the full stat sheet with the Dodge/CDR caps); it isn't offered in the shop, which already shows your gear. Both pause the game; only one is open at a time.
  - **Retry same class** on every end screen (arcade victory/defeat and the career summary) and in the pause menu: same class and same spell picks, straight into wave 1. In career it skips the talents screen too, so newly earned points wait for the next run through Choose class. R keeps going to class select.
  - **Settings** (mode select and pause menu, saved in `user://settings.cfg`, separate from the career save): fullscreen (also F11 / Alt+Enter), screen shake 0–100%, enemy damage numbers on/off (your own red damage numbers and heal texts always show), master volume (ready for when there's audio).
  - The HUD shows the equipped weapon left of the spell bar: its name in its rarity color and a bar that refills between shots.
